# Ketamine Clinic: Questions About Privacy and Information Sharing

Choosing a ketamine clinic involves more than comparing appointment formats or location convenience. One of the most overlooked parts of provider selection is how a clinic handles your personal health information — what it collects, who inside and outside the organization can see it, how long it is kept, and what control you have over sharing it.

This guide focuses narrowly on privacy and information-sharing questions you can ask any ketamine clinic during your research. It is written for people comparing providers, not for anyone seeking individualized medical or legal advice. Privacy rules can vary by jurisdiction, by the type of clinic, and by how a given practice is structured, so treat the questions below as a starting point for a conversation with qualified professionals rather than as a substitute for one.

## Why privacy deserves a place on your provider checklist

When people research ketamine clinics, they often focus on the practical and the clinical: appointment availability, whether visits are in person or remote, how follow-up is handled, and what the intake process looks like. Privacy tends to get pushed to the end of the list, if it appears at all.

That is understandable. Privacy can feel abstract compared with the concrete question of whether a clinic can see you next week. But the information you share with a clinic — your history, your reasons for seeking care, your contact details, and your payment information — is exactly the kind of information that deserves a clear answer about handling before you commit.

Asking privacy questions early also has a practical benefit: the way a clinic responds tells you something about how organized and transparent it is overall. A clinic that can explain its information practices in plain language is often a clinic that has thought carefully about its operations.

## The core question: what information is collected and why

Start with the basics. You want to understand what information a clinic gathers, and what each piece is used for.

Useful questions include:

- What information do you collect before, during, and after a consultation?
- Which parts of that information are required to provide care, and which are optional?
- Do you collect information for purposes beyond my care — for example, marketing, research, or quality improvement?
- If information is used for those other purposes, how is it separated from my care record?

A clinic should be able to explain the difference between information it needs to deliver care and information it collects for other reasons. If those two categories are blurred together in the answer, that is worth noting.

## Who can see your information inside the clinic

"The clinic" is not a single person. It is a group of people with different roles. Ask how access is limited.

- Which staff members can view my record, and is access based on role?
- Are administrative or billing staff able to see clinical notes, or only the minimum they need?
- Do you use any external vendors — for scheduling, records, billing, or communications — and what are their privacy obligations?
- If a vendor is involved, is my information shared with them in identifiable form or in a de-identified form?

This is where the details matter. A clinic that can describe role-based access and vendor obligations is giving you a more complete picture than one that simply says "your information is confidential."

## Hypothetical example: asking about vendor involvement

*The following is a clearly labeled hypothetical example, not a real patient story or a description of any specific clinic.*

Imagine you are comparing two clinics. At the first, you ask whether any outside company handles scheduling reminders. The person you speak with says they are not sure and offers to find out. At the second, the person explains that reminders are sent through a scheduling vendor, that the vendor receives only your name and appointment time, and that clinical notes are not shared with that vendor.

The second answer is more specific. Neither answer is proof of anything on its own, but specificity helps you decide what follow-up questions to ask.

## Hypothetical example: asking about family or caregiver communication

*Again, this is a hypothetical example used only to illustrate the kind of question worth asking.*

Suppose you want a partner or family member to be able to call the clinic and confirm your appointment time, but you do not want that person to receive any clinical information. You could ask: "Can I authorize someone to handle scheduling logistics only, without giving them access to my clinical information?"

A clinic's answer will depend on its own policies and on applicable rules in your jurisdiction. The point of asking is to learn whether the clinic can accommodate a limited authorization, and how it documents that choice.

## Records, retention, and your access

Another set of questions concerns what happens to your information over time.

- How long do you keep records, and what determines that period?
- Can I request a copy of my record, and is there a process and timeline for that?
- Can I request corrections if something is inaccurate?
- What happens to my information if the clinic closes, merges, or changes ownership?

That last question is easy to forget, but it matters. Practices do change hands, and knowing in advance how records would be handled can save confusion later.

## Communication channels: a frequently overlooked area

How a clinic contacts you is part of information sharing. Ask about it directly.

- What channels do you use for appointment reminders, follow-up, and general communication?
- Can I choose which channels are used, or opt out of certain ones?
- Are messages sent in a way that limits what is visible to others who might see my phone or inbox?
- If I contact you through a website form or email, how is that message handled?

If you share a phone or email account with someone else, or if you simply prefer not to have clinical context appear in a message preview, these questions are especially relevant.

## A practical privacy checklist for provider selection

Use this checklist as you research ketamine clinics. You do not need perfect answers to every item, but gaps are worth noticing.

- [ ] The clinic can explain, in plain language, what information it collects and why.
- [ ] The clinic distinguishes between information needed for care and information used for other purposes.
- [ ] The clinic describes who on staff can access your record and how access is limited.
- [ ] The clinic identifies any external vendors involved in scheduling, records, billing, or communications.
- [ ] The clinic explains how it handles requests to share information with a family member or caregiver, including limited authorizations.
- [ ] The clinic describes its record retention practices and how you can request a copy of your record.
- [ ] The clinic explains what would happen to your information if the practice changed ownership or closed.
- [ ] The clinic lets you discuss communication channel preferences.
- [ ] The clinic points you to its written privacy notice or equivalent document.
- [ ] The clinic is comfortable with you asking follow-up questions and does not rush past them.

## How to evaluate the answers you receive

You are not looking for a single "correct" answer, because practices and rules differ. You are looking for clarity, consistency, and willingness to engage.

A few things to pay attention to:

- **Specificity.** Answers that name roles, processes, and documents tend to be more useful than general reassurances.
- **Consistency.** If two people at the same clinic give you very different answers, that is worth a follow-up question.
- **Comfort with questions.** A clinic that welcomes privacy questions is generally easier to work with than one that treats them as an inconvenience.
- **Written documentation.** A written privacy notice or policy gives you something to review carefully rather than rely on memory.

None of these signals is a guarantee. They are inputs to a decision that also includes clinical, logistical, and personal factors.

## Bringing it together

Privacy and information sharing are not side issues in ketamine clinic selection. They are part of the same decision as location, format, and follow-up. The questions in this guide are designed to be asked early, in plain language, and without needing any technical or legal background.

Write down the answers you receive, compare them across clinics, and revisit anything that felt vague. If a question falls outside what a clinic representative can answer, ask to be pointed to the clinic's written policies or to someone who can address it. And where your situation involves legal or clinical specifics, bring those questions to a qualified professional rather than relying on a general guide.
